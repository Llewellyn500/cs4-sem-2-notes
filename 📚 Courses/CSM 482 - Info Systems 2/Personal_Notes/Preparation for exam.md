## 1. The Strategic Role of Information Systems

### What Are Strategic Information Systems?

A **Strategic Information System (SIS)** is any information system that helps an organization gain a competitive advantage. These systems don't just support the business; they fundamentally change its **goals, operations, products, or relationships** with the outside world.

- **Example:** When Citibank introduced ATMs in 1977, it wasn't just a new service; it was a strategic move that changed how people bank and helped Citibank become the largest bank in the U.S. at the time2.
    

### Using IT to Compete: Two Key Models

To find opportunities where IT can provide a competitive edge, we use two main models:

#### A. Porter's Competitive Forces Model

This model identifies five forces that shape competition in any industry. A company must have a strategy to counter them.

1. **Threat of New Entrants:** How easy it is for new competitors to enter your market.
    
2. **Threat of Substitute Products/Services:** How easily customers can switch to a different product that does the same thing.
    
3. **Bargaining Power of Customers:** How much power customers have to drive down prices.
    
4. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers:** How much power your suppliers have to drive up costs.
    
5. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors:** The intensity of competition from other companies in your industry.
    

To fight these forces, businesses use IT to implement five core competitive strategies:

- **Cost Leadership:** Become the lowest-cost producer. IT helps by streamlining processes and managing inventory efficiently.
    
- **Differentiation:** Make your products or services different from and better than competitors'. IT helps by gathering data to understand customer needs better.
    
- **Innovation:** Find new ways of doing business, like creating unique products or making radical changes to processes.
    
- **Growth:** Expand the company's capacity, move into global markets, or offer new products. IT helps manage this expansion.
    
- **Alliance:** Form partnerships with other companies (customers, suppliers, even competitors) to create a shared advantage.
    

#### B. The Value Chain Model

This model views the company as a "chain" of basic activities that add value to its products or services. It helps pinpoint exactly where IT can be used most effectively.

- **Primary Activities:** Directly related to producing and selling the product.
    
    - **Inbound Logistics:** Receiving and storing materials.
        
    - **Operations:** Turning inputs into finished products.
        
    - **Outbound Logistics:** Storing and distributing finished products.
        
    - **Sales and Marketing:** Promoting and selling products.
        
    - **Service:** Maintaining and repairing products after the sale.
        
- **Support Activities:** These make the primary activities possible.
    
    - **Administration & Management:** The firm's infrastructure.
        
    - **Human Resources:** Recruiting, hiring, and training.
        
    - **Technology:** Improving products and processes.
        
    - **Procurement:** Purchasing the inputs.

## 2. Key Strategic Initiatives Using IT

### Building a Customer-Focused Business

The goal is to **keep customers loyal** by anticipating their needs and providing top-quality service. Internet technologies and CRM systems are key tools. They create channels for constant interaction, allowing companies to:

- Track individual customer preferences.
    
- Supply products and information anytime, anywhere.
    
- Encourage customer involvement in product development and service.
    

### Business Process Reengineering (BPR)

BPR is not just about small improvements; it's about **radically redesigning business processes** to achieve dramatic gains in cost, quality, speed, and service.

| Feature             | Business Improvement       | Business Reengineering (BPR) |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Level of Change** | Incremental (small tweaks) | Radical (complete overhaul)  |
| **Starting Point**  | Existing process           | Clean slate                  |
| **Primary Enabler** | Statistical Control        | Information Technology       |
| **Risk**            | Moderate                   | High                         |

### Becoming an Agile Company

Agility is the ability to thrive in rapidly changing global markets. Agile companies use four key strategies:

1. Provide solutions to customers' problems**, not just products.
    
2. **Cooperate** with customers, suppliers, and even competitors to bring products to market quickly.
    
3. Organize to thrive on **change and uncertainty**.
    
4. Leverage the **knowledge of its people** and empower them.
    

### Creating a Virtual Company

A virtual company uses IT to link people, assets, and ideas, creating a flexible organization that isn't limited by physical walls or boundaries42. They form alliances with partners using the Internet, intranets, and extranets to quickly exploit new opportunities.

### Building a Knowledge-Creating Company

This involves creating new business knowledge and quickly building it into products and services.

- **Explicit Knowledge:** Data, documents, and anything written down or stored on a computer.
    
- **Tacit Knowledge:** The "how-to" knowledge that's in people's heads.
    
- **Knowledge Management Systems (KMS):** The goal of a KMS is to help knowledge workers create, organize, and share important business knowledge.
    
    - **Example:** Siemens' **ShareNet** is a website that acts like a chat room and database, allowing 461,000 employees to share their expertise. It added $122 million in sales by helping sales teams find experts within the company to win contracts.

## 3. The Networked Enterprise: Internet, Intranets, and Extranets

### The Telecommunications Revolution

This revolution is driven by rapid changes in technology and a shift from monopolies to competitive markets. Key technology trends include:

- **Open Systems:** Using common standards (like the Internet) so different hardware and software can easily connect and communicate.
    
- **Analog to Digital:** A shift to digital networks, which are faster, carry more information, and have fewer errors than old analog systems.
    
- **Wireless and Fiber-Optics:** Moving from copper wires to faster and higher-capacity fiber-optic lines and wireless technologies like satellite.
    

### The Internet

The Internet is a global network of networks with no central computer or governing body. Businesses use it to achieve six major goals:

1. Generate **new revenue** from online sales.
    
2. **Reduce costs** through online sales and support.
    
3. **Attract new customers** via web marketing.
    
4. **Increase loyalty** of existing customers with better service.
    
5. Develop new **web-based markets** and channels.
    
6. Develop new **information-based products**.
    

### Intranets

An **intranet** is a network _inside_ an organization that uses Internet technologies (like a web browser) to share information, communicate, and support business processes. It's protected from the outside world by firewalls and passwords. Intranets are valuable for:

- **Communication and Collaboration:** Email, chat, discussion groups, and other collaborative tools.
    
- **Web Publishing:** Easily sharing company documents like newsletters and product catalogs.
    
- **Business Operations:** Running critical business applications like order processing on the intranet platform.
    

### Extranets

An **extranet** is created when a company links its intranet to the intranets of its customers, suppliers, or business partners65. This strengthens relationships by giving partners secure access to needed information and enables better collaboration on things like product development and supply chain management66666666.

## 4. Enterprise Business Systems (Cross-Functional Systems)

These are large-scale systems that integrate processes across multiple business functions67.

### Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)

ERP is the

**technological backbone** of a business68. It's a suite of software modules that integrates and automates a company's core internal business processes like finance, manufacturing, distribution, and human resources69696969.

- **Benefits:**
    
    - **Quality and Efficiency:** Improves how internal business processes work70.
        
    - **Decreased Costs:** Reduces transaction processing costs71.
        
    - **Decision Support:** Provides managers a real-time, cross-functional view of the business72.
        
    - **Enterprise Agility:** Breaks down departmental silos, making the organization more flexible73.
        
- **Causes of Failure:** ERP projects are very risky. Failures are often caused by:
    
    - Underestimating the complexity of planning and training74.
        
    - Failure to involve affected employees75.
        
    - Trying to do too much too fast76.
        
    - Insufficient training and data testing77.
        

### Customer Relationship Management (CRM)

The goal of CRM is to provide the company with a

**single, complete view of every customer**, and to provide the customer with a single, complete view of the company78787878. It's a system that integrates and automates

**sales, marketing, and customer service**79.

- **Application Components:**
    
    - **Contact & Account Management:** Tracks all contacts with customers80.
        
    - **Sales:** Provides tools for sales reps to manage their activities, including cross-selling (selling related products) and up-selling (selling more expensive products)81818181.
        
    - **Marketing & Fulfillment:** Automates tasks for marketing campaigns, like qualifying leads82.
        
    - **Customer Service & Support:** Gives service reps the tools and data they need to help customers quickly83.
        
    - **Retention & Loyalty Programs:** Identifies and rewards the most loyal and profitable customers, because retaining a customer is far cheaper than acquiring a new one84848484.
        
- **Causes of Failure:** Over 50% of CRM projects fail to produce results85. The main reason is relying on the IT solution without first making the necessary
    
    **business process changes** and preparing employees and customers86.
    

### Supply Chain Management (SCM)

The supply chain is the network of organizations and processes for procuring materials, transforming them into products, and distributing them87. The goal of SCM is to get the

**right products to the right place at the right time, in the proper quantity, and at an acceptable cost**88.

- **Benefits:**
    
    - Faster, more accurate order processing89.
        
    - Reductions in inventory levels90.
        
    - Quicker time to market91.
        
    - Lower transaction and materials costs92.
        
- **Causes of Failure (Challenges):**
    
    - Lack of proper demand planning and forecasting93.
        
    - Inaccurate data from other systems94.
        
    - Lack of collaboration between departments and with business partners95.
        

### Other Enterprise Systems

- **Enterprise Application Integration (EAI):** This is "middleware" software that connects different enterprise systems (like CRM and ERP) so they can exchange data and work together seamlessly96969696.
    
- **Enterprise Collaboration Systems (ECS):** These are tools that help teams and workgroups **communicate, coordinate, and collaborate** more effectively97979797.

## 5. Electronic Commerce (e-Commerce) Systems

E-commerce is the entire online process of developing, marketing, selling, delivering, servicing, and paying for products and services98.

### Categories of e-Commerce

- **B2C (Business-to-Consumer):** Businesses sell products and services directly to consumers (e.g., Amazon.com)99.
    
- **B2B (Business-to-Business):** Businesses buy, sell, or trade with other businesses (e.g., online auction portals for industrial parts)100100100100.
    
- **C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer):** Consumers buy and sell with each other (e.g., eBay)101.
    

### Essential e-Commerce Processes

1. **Access Control and Security:** Ensures that buyers and sellers are who they say they are and that transactions are secure, using passwords, encryption, and digital certificates102102102102.
    
2. **Profiling and Personalizing:** Gathers data about users to provide a personalized experience, including product recommendations and targeted ads103103103103.
    
3. **Search Management:** Helps customers find the specific product they are looking for on a website104.
    
4. **Content and Catalog Management:** Manages the product information, multimedia, and text on a website105.
    
5. **Workflow Management:** Automates e-commerce processes by defining the rules, tasks, and routing required to complete a transaction106106106106.
    
6. **Event Notification:** Automatically notifies stakeholders (customers, suppliers, managers) about the status of transactions, such as order confirmation and shipping notices107107107107.
    
7. **Collaboration and Trading:** Includes tools like chat, forums, and online marketplaces that support collaboration and trading among buyers and sellers108.
    
8. **Payment:** Manages payment processes, most commonly through credit cards and electronic shopping carts109109109109109109109109109.
    

### "Clicks and Bricks" Strategy

This refers to how a company integrates its e-commerce ("clicks") operations with its traditional physical ("bricks") operations110110110110. The options exist on a spectrum:

- **Separation (Spin-Off):** Create a completely separate e-commerce company.
    
    - **Pros:** Greater focus, flexibility, access to venture funding111.
        
    - **Example:** Barnes & Noble created Barnesandnoble.com as a separate company, which struggled against the more integrated strategies of competitors112112112112.
        
- **Partial Integration (Partnerships & Joint Ventures):** Work with another company to run the e-commerce side.
    
    - **Pros:** Can share brands and buying power without a full merger.
        
    - **Example:** Rite-Aid's partnership with Drugstore.com113; KB Toys' joint venture for Kbtoys.com114.
        
- **Full Integration (In-House Division):** E-commerce is just another division of the company, fully integrated with other operations.
    
    - **Pros:** Established brand, shared information, purchasing leverage, distribution efficiencies115.
        
    - **Example:** Office Depot successfully integrated OfficeDepot.com with its stores and catalog business, allowing customers to order online and pick up in-store116116116116.

## 6. Functional Business Systems

These are information systems that support the specific activities within a single business function.

### Marketing Systems

- **Interactive Marketing:** Using the internet to create a two-way dialogue with customers, making them partners in product creation and marketing117.
    
- **Targeted Marketing:** Aiming advertising at specific groups based on five components118:
    
    1. **Community:** People with shared interests (e.g., sports fans)119.
        
    2. **Content:** Placing ads on websites with related content120.
        
    3. **Context:** Ads that are relevant to what the user is currently doing121.
        
    4. **Demographic/Psychographic:** Targeting based on age, income, lifestyle, etc.122.
        
    5. **Online Behavior:** Using tracking tools like cookies to tailor ads to an individual's browsing history123.
        
- **Sales Force Automation:** Equipping salespeople with laptops and software to increase their productivity and speed up the reporting of sales data124124124124.
    

### Manufacturing Systems

- **Computer-Integrated Manufacturing (CIM):** A philosophy that stresses using computers to **simplify, automate, and integrate** all production processes125. Key technologies include:
    
    - **CAD/CAE:** Computer-Aided Design/Engineering to design better products126.
        
    - **MRP:** Material Requirements Planning to plan the materials needed for production127.
        
    - **CAM:** Computer-Aided Manufacturing to automate the production process itself128.
        
    - **MES (Manufacturing Execution Systems):** Monitor and control activities on the factory floor129.
        

### Human Resource (HRM) Systems

HRM systems support recruitment, placement, compensation, and development of employees130. The Internet and corporate intranets have transformed HRM by:

- Enabling
    
    **online recruiting** through corporate websites and job sites like Monster.com131131131131.
    
- Allowing for
    
    **Employee Self-Service (ESS)** on intranets, where employees can view benefits, update personal information, and access training materials132.
    

### Accounting and Financial Systems

- **Accounting Systems:** The oldest and most widely used IS in business133. They record and report transactions. Common systems include Order Processing, Inventory Control, Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, Payroll, and General Ledger134.
    
- **Financial Management Systems:** Support decisions about financing the business and allocating financial resources135. They help with cash management, investment decisions, and capital budgeting (evaluating the profitability of proposed big-ticket purchases)136.
