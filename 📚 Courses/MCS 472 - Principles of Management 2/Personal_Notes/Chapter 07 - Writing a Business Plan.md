# 📘 Study Guide: Writing a Business Plan (MGT 372)

---

## 1. **What is a Business Plan?**

- **Definition:** A written narrative (25–35 pages) describing how a new business will achieve its goals.
    
- **Dual Purpose:**
    
    - **Internal:** Forces the founding team to systematically think through the business.
        
    - **External:** Communicates venture merits to investors, banks, and partners.
        

👉 **Analogy:** A business plan is like a **blueprint for a house**—builders (employees) and investors (financiers) both need it to see how the house will be built.

---

## 2. **Who Reads a Business Plan?**

1. **Employees/Stakeholders (internal):** Helps them work in sync.
    
2. **Investors/External Stakeholders:** Looking for credibility, profitability, and sustainability.
    

---

## 3. **Guidelines for Writing a Business Plan**

- Use a **conventional structure** (investors prefer clarity over creativity).
    
- Keep it **25–35 pages**: enough detail but concise.
    
- Recognize the plan will **change over time** (feedback, new insights).
    

👉 **Analogy:** Think of a business plan like a **CV**—it must be structured, easy to scan, and convincing.

---

## 4. **Types of Business Plans**

1. **Summary Business Plan** – short, for testing interest.
    
2. **Full Business Plan** – detailed, for investors.
    
3. **Operational Business Plan** – internal roadmap.
    

---

## 5. **Suggested Outline of a Business Plan**

### 1. **Cover Page & Table of Contents**

- Basic information for navigation.
    

### 2. **Executive Summary**

- 1–2 pages, most important part.
    
- Written last but appears first.
    
- Should convince investors to read the full plan.
    

👉 **Analogy:** Like a movie trailer—if it’s not exciting, no one will watch the full movie.

---

### 3. **Industry Analysis**

- Industry size, growth, trends, key players, success factors.
    

---

### 4. **Company Description**

- Background, mission, products/services, ownership, partners.
    
- Shows ability to turn an **idea into a real business**.
    

---

### 5. **Market Analysis**

- Segmentation, target market, consumer behavior, competitor analysis.  
    👉 **Tip:** Focus on a niche; don’t try to serve the entire industry.
    

---

### 6. **Economics of the Business**

- Revenue drivers, profit margins, fixed & variable costs, break-even analysis.  
    👉 **Analogy:** Like planning a trip—know how much fuel (cost) and distance (sales) before reaching your destination (profit).
    

---

### 7. **Marketing Plan**

- Strategy (product, price, promotion, distribution).
    
- Sales cycle: Prospecting → Approach → Presentation → Closing → Follow-up.
    

---

### 8. **Design & Development Plan**

- Status of product/service development.
    
- Risks, costs, IP protection.
    

---

### 9. **Operations Plan**

- Daily operations: facilities, location, back-stage vs. front-stage activities.
    

👉 _Back-stage:_ manufacturing, supply chain.  
👉 _Front-stage:_ customer service, sales.

---

### 10. **Management Team & Structure**

- Founders, management, board of directors/advisers.  
    👉 **Note:** Investors often skip to this section after the executive summary.
    

---

### 11. **Overall Schedule**

- Milestones: incorporation, prototype, financing, first sale.
    

---

### 12. **Financial Projections**

- Sources & uses of funds.
    
- Pro forma income statement, cash flow, balance sheet, ratio analysis.
    

👉 **Analogy:** Like a **scoreboard in football**—it shows the outcome of all plays (business activities).

---

### 13. **Appendices**

- Supporting documents: brochures, industry reports, contracts, maps, articles.
    

---

## 6. **Presenting a Business Plan to Investors**

- **Oral Presentation Rules:**
    
    - Follow time limits.
        
    - Keep slides simple & clear.
        
    - Be well-rehearsed.
        
- **12 Key Slides for Investor Pitch:**
    
    1. Title
        
    2. Problem
        
    3. Solution
        
    4. Opportunity & Target Market
        
    5. Technology
        
    6. Competition
        
    7. Marketing & Sales
        
    8. Management Team
        
    9. Financial Projections
        
    10. Current Status
        
    11. Funding Required
        
    12. Summary
        

👉 **Analogy:** Investor presentation is like a **first date**—make a good impression, be confident, but don’t overshare.

---

✅ **Exam Tip:**  
When asked to “outline a business plan,” write the **13 major sections** in order with **2–3 points each**. If asked about **purpose**, stress both **internal (planning)** and **external (funding/credibility)** roles.
