# 📘 Study Guide: Industry and Competitor Analysis (MGT 372)

---

## 1. **What is Industry Analysis?**

- **Industry:** A group of firms producing similar products/services (e.g., solar panel makers, yoga studios).
    
- **Industry Analysis:** Business research that evaluates the **potential of an industry**.
    

👉 **Why Important?**

- Determines if the target market (from feasibility analysis) is favorable.
    
- Helps new ventures know whether entering the industry is realistic and profitable.
    

**Three Key Questions to Ask:**

1. Is the industry **accessible** for a new venture?
    
2. Are there **positions** in the industry that avoid its negative aspects?
    
3. Does it contain **markets ripe for innovation or underserved niches**?
    

---

## 2. **The Five Forces Model (Michael Porter)**

Framework to analyze **industry profitability**.  
Think of it as **five walls pressing in on profits**—firms must push back to survive.

### 1. **Threat of Substitutes**

- High when customers can easily switch to alternatives.
    
- Example: Soft drinks vs. fruit juices.
    
- Few substitutes = higher profits (e.g., prescription drugs).  
    👉 _Analogy:_ Like having many different restaurants nearby—customers can easily switch if one raises prices.
    

---

### 2. **Threat of New Entrants**

- Profitable industries attract new players → lowers profits.
    
- **Barriers to Entry:**
    
    - Economies of scale
        
    - Strong brand identity (e.g., Coca-Cola, Pepsi)
        
    - Capital requirements (banks, airlines)
        
    - Government regulation (e.g., banking licenses in Ghana)
        
- **Non-traditional barriers:** first-mover advantage, strong management, unique business models.  
    👉 _Analogy:_ Like a **fortress**—the stronger the walls (barriers), the harder it is for outsiders to get in.
    

---

### 3. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors**

- Main determinant of profitability.
    
- Fierce rivalry = lower profits.
    
- Factors:
    
    - Number & balance of competitors
        
    - Degree of product differentiation
        
    - Growth rate of industry (slow growth = more rivalry)
        
    - High fixed costs = more aggressive selling.  
        👉 _Analogy:_ Like a crowded marketplace where all sellers shout and drop prices to attract buyers.
        

---

### 4. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers**

- Suppliers raise prices or lower quality → reduces profits.
    
- Strong when:
    
    - Few suppliers exist
        
    - Switching costs are high
        
    - No substitutes available
        
    - Suppliers can “forward integrate” (enter buyers’ industry).  
        👉 _Analogy:_ Like a landlord with few apartments in town—tenants have no choice but to pay more.
        

---

### 5. **Bargaining Power of Buyers**

- Buyers demand lower prices or higher quality.
    
- Strong when:
    
    - Few buyers purchase from many suppliers (e.g., carmakers vs. auto part suppliers).
        
    - Products are standardized
        
    - Buyers can “backward integrate” (make their own supply).  
        👉 _Analogy:_ Like a bulk buyer at the market—because they buy in large amounts, they negotiate lower prices.
        

---

## 3. **Applications of the Five Forces Model**

1. **Assess Industry Attractiveness:** If too many threats are high, profitability will be low.
    
2. **Strategic Positioning:** Firms can choose niches that minimize these forces.
    

👉 Example: A coffee shop adds Wi-Fi and comfort to reduce substitute threats.

---

## 4. **Types of Industries & Opportunities**

1. **Emerging Industries** – standards not set → **first-mover advantage**.
    
2. **Fragmented Industries** – many small firms → opportunity for **consolidation**.
    
3. **Mature Industries** – slow demand growth → **innovation** and **after-sales service**.
    
4. **Declining Industries** – shrinking demand → **cost leadership**, niche strategy.
    
5. **Global Industries** – international reach → **multidomestic or global strategies**.
    

👉 _Analogy:_ Industries are like **stages of life**—childhood (emerging), youth (fragmented), adulthood (mature), old age (declining).

---

## 5. **Competitor Analysis**

- **Definition:** A detailed evaluation of competitors to understand their position.
    
- Helps identify **strengths, weaknesses, and gaps** to exploit.
    

**Tools & Sources of Competitive Intelligence:**

- Conferences & trade shows
    
- Competitors’ websites & social media
    
- Buying and testing competitor products
    
- Customer feedback
    
- Industry reports, Google Alerts
    

**Competitive Analysis Grid:**

- A table comparing your firm vs. competitors on key factors (pricing, service, innovation).
    
- Helps see where you can differentiate.
    

👉 _Analogy:_ Like a **football coach studying rivals’ tactics** before a match.
