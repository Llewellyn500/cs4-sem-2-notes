# 📘 Study Guide: Franchising (MGT 372)

---

## 1. **What is Franchising?**

- **Definition:**  
    A form of business organization where a **franchisor** (owner of a successful business) licenses its **brand, trademark, and method of doing business** to a **franchisee** in exchange for a fee and ongoing royalties.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Global: McDonald’s, KFC, Subway.
        
    - Ghana: Papaye, Chicken Inn, and Melcom Express outlets.
        

👉 **Analogy:**  
Franchising is like **renting a recipe**—the franchisor provides the recipe and brand, while the franchisee cooks and sells under that brand.

---

## 2. **Types of Franchise Systems**

1. **Product & Trademark Franchise** – Franchisee sells franchisor’s product and uses its name.
    
    - Example: Coca-Cola bottlers, GM car dealers.
        
2. **Business Format Franchise** – Franchisor provides full business system: training, manuals, ads, ongoing support.
    
    - Example: Fast-food chains (KFC, Burger King, Pizza Hut).
        
    - _Most popular form for entrepreneurs._
        

👉 **Analogy:**  
Product franchise = “borrowing someone’s ingredients.”  
Business format franchise = “borrowing their full kitchen and cookbook.”

---

## 3. **Types of Franchise Agreements**

- **Individual Franchise Agreement** – Single outlet.
    
- **Area Franchise Agreement** – Rights to open multiple outlets in a region.
    
- **Master Franchise Agreement** – Authority to recruit other franchisees in a region.
    

---

## 4. **When is Franchising Most Appropriate?**

- Works best when:
    
    - Strong trademark/brand.
        
    - Well-designed business model.
        
    - Desire for rapid growth.
        
- Doesn’t work well for **large, complex businesses** (e.g., Walmart).
    

---

## 5. **Steps in Setting Up a Franchise System**

1. Develop a solid business concept.
    
2. Register trademarks and legal protections.
    
3. Create operating manuals.
    
4. Develop a training program.
    
5. Establish support systems.
    
6. Prepare marketing strategy.
    
7. Recruit and select franchisees.
    
8. Grow gradually to maintain control.
    
9. Monitor performance.
    

---

## 6. **Qualities of a Good Franchisee**

- Good work ethic.
    
- Ability to follow instructions.
    
- Team-oriented.
    
- Industry experience.
    
- Adequate financial resources.
    
- Positive representation of brand.
    

👉 **Analogy:**  
A franchisee is like a **driver of someone else’s bus**—must follow the route, but can still bring energy, discipline, and skill.

---

## 7. **Advantages & Disadvantages**

### For the Franchisor (expanding via franchise)

✅ Advantages:

- Rapid, low-cost market expansion.
    
- Franchise fees & royalties = steady income.
    
- Franchisee motivation.
    
- Access to new ideas.
    
- Cost savings (bulk buying).
    

❌ Disadvantages:

- Profit sharing.
    
- Loss of control.
    
- Conflicts with franchisees.
    
- Legal expenses.
    
- Managing rapid growth.
    

---

### For the Franchisee (buying a franchise)

✅ Advantages:

- Proven business system.
    
- Established brand recognition.
    
- Training & support.
    
- Marketing & network benefits.
    
- Easier access to financing.
    

❌ Disadvantages:

- High costs (fees, royalties).
    
- Limited creativity.
    
- Long-term commitment.
    
- Risk of fraud/mismanagement by franchisor.
    
- Dependence on brand reputation (if others fail, it affects you).
    

👉 **Analogy:**  
Being a franchisee is like **joining a football club**—you get training, a uniform, and a team strategy, but you can’t just play your own style freely.

---

## 8. **Costs of Buying a Franchise**

- **Initial Franchise Fee** – upfront payment.
    
- **Capital Requirements** – land, buildings, licenses, inventory.
    
- **Continuing Royalty Payments** – usually ~5% of monthly revenue.
    
- **Advertising Fees** – contributions to national/regional campaigns.
    
- **Other Fees** – staff training, IT systems, management support.
    

---

## 9. **Steps in Purchasing a Franchise**

1. Self-evaluation (are you willing to follow rules, take orders?).
    
2. Research franchise opportunities.
    
3. Evaluate franchisor’s track record & disclosure documents (FDD).
    
4. Interview existing franchisees.
    
5. Arrange financing.
    
6. Sign agreement.
    
7. Attend training & set up operations.
    

---

## 10. **Legal Aspects**

- Governed by laws & regulations (FTC in U.S., Trade Marks Act in Ghana).
    
- Franchisor must provide **Franchisor Disclosure Document (FDD)** with details of fees, obligations, and support.
    

---

## 11. **Other Issues**

- **Franchise Ethics:** Beware of “get-rich-quick” mentality and conflicts between franchisor & franchisees.
    
- **International Franchising:** Growing due to globalization. Consider:
    
    - Brand value abroad.
        
    - Local laws.
        
    - Product demand.
        
    - Support & training available.
        

👉 **Analogy:**  
International franchising is like **transplanting a tree**—it must be adapted to the new soil (market conditions) to survive.
