# 📘 Study Guide: Getting Funding and Financing (MGT 372)

---

## 1. **Why Do New Ventures Need Financing?**

Most startups need funding early because of:

1. **Cash Flow Challenges** – Expenses come before revenues (rent, salaries, suppliers).
    
2. **Capital Investments** – Buying equipment, facilities, licenses.
    
3. **Lengthy Product Development Cycles** – Need funds before first sales.
    

👉 **Analogy:** Starting a business is like planting a farm—you need money for seeds, tools, and labor before you harvest.

---

## 2. **Sources of Financing**

### a) **Personal Financing**

1. **Personal Funds** – Most founders use savings.
    
2. **Friends & Family** – Common but risky if business fails.
    
3. **Bootstrapping** – Creative cost-saving to avoid external funds.
    
    - Examples: buy used equipment, lease instead of buy, advance payments from customers, share office space, hire interns.
        

👉 **Analogy:** Bootstrapping is like **fixing a car with duct tape**—cheap and creative solutions until you can afford better.

---

### b) **Equity Financing** (ownership-based)

- Selling shares/ownership in exchange for funds.
    

**Main Sources:**

1. **Business Angels** – Wealthy individuals investing $10k–$500k in startups.
    
    - Look for 30–40% growth potential.
        
    - Example: Early backers of Facebook and WhatsApp.
        
2. **Venture Capital (VC)** – Professional firms investing pooled money from institutions.
    
    - Invest in high-growth, scalable businesses.
        
    - Provide not just money but also mentorship and networks.
        
    - Enter later than angels.
        
3. **Initial Public Offering (IPO)** – First sale of stock to the public.
    
    - Motivations: raise equity capital, boost public profile, allow investors to cash out, use stock as “currency” for acquisitions.
        

👉 **Analogy:**

- Angel = supportive uncle giving you startup cash.
    
- VC = corporate banker who expects big returns.
    
- IPO = opening your business for everyone to buy shares like a supermarket sale.
    

---

### c) **Debt Financing** (loan-based)

- Borrowed money, must be repaid with interest.
    

**Sources:**

1. **Commercial Banks** – Rare for startups (risk-averse, prefer established firms).
    
2. **SBA Guaranteed Loans** – Government-backed loans.
    
3. **Vendor Credit** – Buy now, pay later from suppliers.
    
4. **Factoring** – Sell receivables to get quick cash.
    
5. **Merchant Cash Advance** – Lender takes % of future sales (very costly).
    
6. **Peer-to-Peer Lending** – Online loans between individuals (e.g., Lending Club, Funding Circle).
    

👉 **Analogy:** Debt financing is like **borrowing your friend’s bicycle**—you must return it, possibly with a small gift (interest).

---

### d) **Creative Financing Sources**

1. **Leasing** – Use assets with little upfront cost (e.g., vehicles, equipment).
    
    - More expensive long-term but reduces immediate burden.
        
2. **Crowdfunding** – Raising funds from many people online.
    
    - **Rewards-based:** Offer perks (Kickstarter, Indiegogo).
        
    - **Equity-based:** Investors get ownership (FundersClub, Crowdfunder).
        
3. **Strategic Partners** – Larger firms co-invest for shared benefits.
    
    - Example: Biotech startups partnering with pharma companies for clinical trials.
        

👉 **Analogy:** Crowdfunding is like **passing a hat at a concert**—many people contribute small amounts to support you.

---

## 3. **Preparing to Raise Debt or Equity Financing**

1. **Determine how much is needed** and for what purpose.
    
2. **Match financing type** with venture characteristics (e.g., tech startup → equity; retail shop → debt).
    
3. **Prepare an Elevator Speech** – 60 seconds to sell your idea:
    
    - 20s: Problem/opportunity.
        
    - 20s: How product solves it.
        
    - 10s: Your qualifications.
        
    - 10s: Market potential.
        

👉 **Analogy:** An elevator pitch is like a **movie teaser trailer**—short but must grab attention.

---

## 4. **Advantages & Disadvantages**

### Equity Financing

✅ No repayment obligation, access to expertise.  
❌ Dilution of ownership, loss of some control.

### Debt Financing

✅ Ownership retained, predictable payments.  
❌ Repayment pressure, risk if sales are low.

### Creative Financing

✅ Flexibility, alternative when banks/VCs reject.  
❌ Often higher costs or dependency on partners.
